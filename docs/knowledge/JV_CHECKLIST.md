# JV deals: questions, red flags and a safe agreement

How we work a joint venture (JV) with another wholesaler who already has a property under contract.
The pipeline order is decision D-048. This page is the "what to ask and what to sign" companion.
Not legal advice: the closing title company's attorney reviews the agreement before signing.

## 1. What the holder wants (and why they post a JV)

A holder posts a JV because they have a contract and a deadline but no buyer at their price.
They want three things, in this order:

1. **Close before the deadline** (or they lose the deal and their reputation with the seller).
2. **Get paid** (their share of the assignment fee).
3. **Not get cut out** (they fear we go around them to the seller).

So our message should say: we have a matched buyer in that area, we close through title, and we sign a JV
with non-circumvention both ways. That answers all three.

## 2. Seller motivation: what to ask the holder

The holder knows why the seller is selling. That tells us how firm the price and date are.

- Why is the owner selling? (inherited, behind on payments, tired landlord, divorce, relocation, repairs they can't afford)
- Is the house vacant or occupied? Tenant on a lease?
- Is there a foreclosure sale date or tax sale date?
- Is the seller flexible on the closing date?

## 3. Questions for the holder before we show the deal to a buyer

Ask these in the first reply. Missing answers stay missing on the card.

1. **Contract:** "Do you have it under contract, signed by every owner on title? Is it assignable? What is your contract price and closing date?"
2. **Your terms:** "What is your split or fee (for example 50/50 of the spread, or a set fee on top of your price)?"
3. **Title:** "Which title company or closing attorney has the file? Has the seller signed their docs?"
4. **Earnest money:** "Is your earnest money deposited? How much does the end buyer need to put down, and by when?"
5. **Access:** "Photos and video? Lockbox or appointment? Can the buyer walk it before EMD?"
6. **Condition:** "Roof, HVAC, foundation, water heater, plumbing and electrical age? Known issues?"
7. **Numbers:** "What comps did you use for your ARV?" (we always use our own comps; theirs are a claim)
8. **Other JV partners:** "Is anyone else marketing it? Is there a daisy chain (you assigned from another wholesaler)?"
9. **Deadline:** "When do you need a signed buyer by?"
10. **Contact:** "Best phone and email for you, and can we get on your buyers list?"

## 4. Red flags (slow down or walk away)

- Won't share a copy of the contract (with the price) before a buyer introduction.
- "Comment for address", but the address doesn't match the photos or the county record.
- Owner on the county record doesn't match the seller on the contract.
- Asking price above our ARV minus repairs minus the buyer's margin (check the buyer's max on the card).
- Earnest money to be paid to the wholesaler instead of the title company.
- Closing in fewer than 7 days with no title file opened.
- The same house posted by several people at different prices (daisy chain).
- A brand-new profile, no history in the group, asks for a deposit or a "reservation fee".

| 4. Buyer interested | We ask: "At $X, cash, close by <date>, EMD $Y to title. Yes?" |
| 5. JV signed | Nothing new yet. |
| 6. Full package | Address, photos, access, comps with links, assignment terms. |
| 7. EMD at title | Buyer signs the assignment and wires EMD to the title company. |

## 6. The JV agreement: must-have clauses

A JV agreement protects both partners. Ours has these, and we don't sign without them:

1. **Property and contract identified** (address, seller, holder's contract date, price, closing date, title file number).
2. **Contract copy verified** before the agreement takes effect: signed by all owners of record, assignable, in force.
3. **Co-holder interest:** the holder assigns an undivided interest in the purchaser's rights to the JV, so each partner acts as a principal selling their own contract interest. (Florida, chapter 475: an unlicensed person may sell only their own contract interest. Texas §1101.0045 requires disclosure that we sell a contract interest, not the property. North Carolina has similar rules. Check the state rules table.)
4. **Fee split in writing** (dollar amount or percent of the net spread) and **paid only by the title company at closing**, on the settlement statement, through an **irrevocable disbursement authorization** both partners sign.
5. **No closing, no fee.** Nobody owes anything if it doesn't close.
6. **Earnest money only with the title company.**
7. **Non-circumvention and non-solicitation, 24 months, both ways:** neither partner contacts the other's seller, buyer or property directly, or sells them other deals, without paying the agreed share. A breach owes the full agreed fee plus attorney fees.
8. **Confidentiality** of contacts and terms.
9. **Who does what:** holder keeps the contract alive, handles the seller and access; we bring the buyer and manage the buyer's EMD and closing.
10. **Deadline and end:** ends at closing or the contract deadline plus written extensions.
11. **Taxes:** each partner pays their own. Gabriel is a foreign person (D-045): the title company handles any withholding forms.
12. **Electronic signatures count.** Governing law: the property's state.

The fill-in template lives in Gabriel's private folder (`exports/assistant/JV-agreement-DRAFT.md`). When the dashboard generates agreements (#288), it uses these clauses.

## 7. Why we don't let the buyer "message the holder directly"

If the buyer and holder talk before the JV is signed, both can close without us. The JV agreement with
non-circumvention comes first (stage 5). After that, a group chat with all three is fine.
