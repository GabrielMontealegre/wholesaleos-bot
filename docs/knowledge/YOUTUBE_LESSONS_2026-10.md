## What we adopt

### 1. Motivation ranking = our Black Diamond signal stacking (video 1)
The signals they rank by match ours: out-of-state owner, pre-foreclosure, repairs needed, long ownership, tax
delinquency. They spend the effort on better data, not on more calls. **Use:** #275 tiers. Add a
**pre-call brief** on each top card, built only from our own records: the lead's timeline, signals, value,
deadline, and the last conversation. No people-search.

### 2. Offer on the call (video 1)
Three inputs while on the phone: address, condition, the seller's number. The repair rule of thumb they use:
**light cosmetic $15–25 per sq ft, full interior remodel $25–50 per sq ft**, plus named items. Minimum
wholesale fee about $10k. The tool also writes a short, seller-friendly explanation of the offer.
**Use:** Deal Check calculator presets (labeled "estimate", never a gate) and a "say it like this" line.

### 3. One deal → five buyer messages (video 1)
Email, text, Facebook group, Instagram and letter, each short and numbers-first ("off-market, X% of ARV,
area, ARV, repairs, price"). **Use:** the #288 deal-package generator outputs these five, and Gabriel
approves them.

### 4. Public records requests by email (video 5)
An agent emails every county's code-enforcement office (all 67 Florida counties) asking for the last 30 days
of open code violations. It follows up on the ones that don't reply and imports the CSVs that come back.
These are **public records requests**: legal, free and repeatable monthly.
**Use:** a "Records requests" tracker (county, office, what was asked, sent, follow-up, reply, file
imported). The dashboard drafts the emails, Gabriel approves the batch, and his Gmail sends them. Replies are
linked and the files are imported with their provenance. Covers code violations, tax-delinquent lists and
pre-foreclosure filings where an office hands them out on request.

### 5. On-market done right (video 4)
- Check **sold** first: is anyone buying here?
- Then search active houses only (no new construction, foreclosures or auctions), **listed 30+ days**,
  plus **one or two keywords at a time**: as-is, fixer upper, TLC, motivated, opportunity, investor special.
  Look for price cuts and ugly photos.
- An offer must be far under list (about **$30k+ below**, rule of thumb). Skip low-equity houses: the
  loan must be paid off, and short sales can't be wholesaled. We still never infer a payoff (§5). Equity
  stays a sorting band only (D-011).
- **Stack:** an active listing that is also vacant, inherited, in pre-foreclosure or tax-delinquent means
  a motivated seller that other buyers don't know about. **Use:** when Gabriel saves a listing (Chrome panel,
  A-019), the dashboard checks our county records for the same parcel and flags the stack.
- Agents become lead sources: text them three short sentences. A "no" today can be a "yes" in 30–60 days,
  so follow up. **Use:** an agent pipeline with 30/60-day follow-ups.

### 6. Land: market and parcel checks (video 2)
- **Market:** at least ~200 land sales a year in the county; more sold than active; sell-through about
  60% (6 months) and 120% (1 year); median price per acre roughly $10k–50k. Days on market barely
  matters. Avoid saturated builder markets.
- **Builder buyers:** houses sold with year built = this year show spec builders. The LLC that bought the
  lot is a land buyer. **Use:** extends B-09 to land buyers.
- **List filters:** 0.2–100 acres, owned 10+ years, no corporate owners, not landlocked, some road
  frontage, limited wetlands or flood. Campaigns of about 1,000 leads; group neighbor counties.
- **Parcel checklist** before an offer: wetlands, flood zone, adjacent parcels with the same owner, soil
  (septic), road frontage, slope, surface water. **Use:** free official layers on every land card: FEMA
  flood map, the National Wetlands Inventory, USDA soil survey and county GIS.
- **Exits:** assign to a builder; or list on the MLS with an agent and double close with a transactional
  lender. A double close only makes sense with a big spread.

## What we do NOT copy (our rules)
- People-search sites to get phones (video 6). The system never does this (§5). Gabriel may look up
  owners himself.
- Agents that "bypass robot checkers" (video 5). Never (§5).
- AI cold calls. They're illegal (the video itself says so). Auto-texting sellers: no (D-007); texts are
  operator-sent batches of at most 20, and the Texas texting rule (A-017) applies.
- Pretext openers (e.g. a "neighbor" note about a fake problem). Misleading; we stay honest.
- Hiding that we're wholesalers where a state requires disclosure (TX §1101.0045). Follow the state rules
  table.
